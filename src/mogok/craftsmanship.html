Cutting & craftsmanship

Cutting your own material is not a romantic detail. It changes which decisions are available to you, and it is the reason we can answer questions about a stone that a dealer buying finished goods simply cannot.

  1. Sorting

    Rough arrives from our workings and from the miners we buy from. Each piece is judged for the colour it will show once cut — not the colour it shows now — for what is hidden behind the skin, and for how much of its weight will survive a shape. Material that will not carry a faceted cut is set aside for cabochon rather than forced.

  2. Orienting

    Corundum is doubly refractive and Mogok material is often colour-zoned. Where the table is placed relative to the crystal axis decides the face-up colour, and it is a decision that cannot be revisited. In star sapphire the same decision decides whether the star sits centred or off to one side.

  3. Cutting

    The trade-off is almost always weight against colour. A shallower crown holds colour through the table; a deeper pavilion holds the velvety character in sapphire; both cost carats. A cutter working for a client who paid by the gram chooses weight. A cutter working for the house that will have to sell the stone chooses the stone. That is the whole argument for cutting your own material.

  4. Polishing and finishing

    Facet junctions, symmetry, and the polish itself. Poor finishing is the fault most easily hidden by photography and most obvious in the hand, which is why we will always show you a stone on camera under moving light before you commit.

  5. Setting

    The bench sits alongside the cutters. In practice that means the person setting a stone can ask the person who cut it why the pavilion is the depth it is — and a setting can be designed in section around the actual stone rather than around a calibrated size the stone has been ground down to fit.

A rare asset

Rough, then cut

Because we cut our own material, we can photograph a piece of rough and then photograph the finished stone that came out of it. Almost no competitor can produce that pair, for the simple reason that almost no competitor was there for the first half.

Where the rough photograph exists, the stone's page shows the two side by side. It is the single most persuasive thing we can put in front of a buyer who is trying to decide whether we are what we say we are.
