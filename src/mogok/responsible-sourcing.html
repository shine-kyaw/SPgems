Sourcing

Responsible sourcing

This page used to be a list of adjectives. We have replaced it with a list of documents, because a procurement team can act on the second and not the first.

What we changed, and why

Our previous site described our materials as “eco-friendly, ethically sourced, fair-trade and responsible,” and said that “conflict-free simply isn't good enough.” It named no standard, no scheme, no auditor and no certification.

We have removed all of it. Three reasons, in ascending order of importance. First, sophisticated buyers discount unsupported ethics claims and often read them as a negative signal. Second, “fair-trade” has a specific certification meaning in several jurisdictions and using it without holding that certification may be a misleading commercial claim. Third, and decisively: in the Myanmar context, vague ethics language invites scrutiny that it cannot survive, whereas specific chain-of-custody documentation withstands it.

What we do document

For every stone we sell, we hold and will produce on request:

  • The purchase record, dated, for material bought as rough from independent miners.
  • The laboratory report, where one has been issued, with its number published openly on the stone's page so you can verify it at the laboratory rather than taking our word for it.
  • Export documentation and customs declarations for the shipment.
  • Our own record of which of the three sourcing routes the stone came through, marked on its catalogue entry.

Where a stone came out of our own workings, we say so on its page, and that is the strongest origin statement we are able to make about anything. Where it did not, we do not imply otherwise.

What we will not claim

  • We do not hold a fair-trade certification, so we do not use the term.
  • We do not claim to be carbon-neutral, eco-friendly, or sustainable. Gem mining moves a great deal of rock to recover very little, and describing that as environmentally benign would be untrue.
  • We do not name clients. We have been asked to keep those relationships private and we will, which means we also will not gesture at “celebrities and public figures” without naming anyone. That gesture was on our previous site. It has gone.

Compliance

The regulatory position

Myanmar-origin gemstones sit inside an active, entity-based international sanctions regime. Any buyer outside Myanmar should assume their own jurisdiction has a view, and many will have compliance obligations of their own.

We do not operate an unattended checkout, and this is one of the reasons. Every enquiry is reviewed by a person before any commitment is made, and the buyer's country is asked at the point of enquiry precisely so that review can happen.

Community

In Mogok

We ran an environmental programme in Mogok in 2016. It exists, it was photographed, and it was published on our old site as nine separate blog posts with identical titles, which is not a record of anything — it is a broken photo gallery.

A specific, dated, evidenced account of one real programme in one named community is worth more than any amount of “eco-friendly.” We would rather publish that properly than publish nine copies of a headline.

Needs confirmation from SP Gems

What the 2016 programme actually did, where, with whom, and what came of it. Also: whether SP Gems holds membership of any trade association (ICA, AGTA, the Myanmar Gems and Jewellery Entrepreneurs Association), and whether anyone in the business holds a stated gemmological credential such as GIA GG or FGA.
