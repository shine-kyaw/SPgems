Other Gemstones

Mogok is a corundum deposit, but it is not only a corundum deposit. The same valley produces peridot in a saturated grass green that most commercial material never reaches, strongly dichroic tourmaline, and moonstone with a clean blue sheen. These are cut in the same workshop, to the same standard, and priced without the premium that the word “ruby” attaches to everything near it.

About the rest of the valley

Good stones without the famous name

Mogok peridot is worth singling out. Most peridot on the market carries an olive cast; the Mogok deposits produce a yellowish green with real saturation, and in sizes that hold it. It is a stone that photographs poorly and shows well in the hand, which is a combination the internet has not been kind to.

Tourmaline from here is strongly dichroic — the colour shifts down the length of the crystal — so the cut has to be arranged around the orientation rather than the outline. Step cuts do that better than brilliants, which is why you will see them here.

These are also the stones we recommend to buyers who want something genuinely from Mogok without a five-figure commitment. They are cut by the same hands as the ruby.
