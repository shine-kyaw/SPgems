International purchasing & compliance

If you are buying Myanmar-origin material from outside Myanmar, there are questions you should be asking. Rather than wait for you to ask them, here they are, with the honest state of our answers.

Requires professional review before publication

Everything on this page must be reviewed and approved by counsel with sanctions expertise before it goes live. It is written here to establish the structure and the register — what a buyer needs to see, and in what tone. It is not legal advice, it is not a substitute for it, and it should not be published as drafted.

The position, plainly

Myanmar-origin gemstones sit inside an active international sanctions environment. The current frameworks are entity-based rather than origin-based — that is, they target named entities rather than banning material from the country outright — but the practical picture is more complicated than that sentence makes it sound, and reasonable people in the trade disagree about where the line falls.

We are not going to pretend otherwise, and we are not going to tell you what your own obligations are. What we will do is be specific about what we hold and what we can produce, so that you or your compliance team can make your own assessment.

What you should establish before you buy

  • Whether you can lawfully import into your own jurisdiction. This depends on your country and, in some cases, on your own status. It is your call and your adviser's, not ours.
  • What documentation your customs authority will require. Requirements differ substantially, and it is much easier to assemble a document set before shipping than to produce it at a border.
  • What your own buyers or compliance team will require. If you are a designer or a dealer, your client's procurement team may have standards of its own.

What we hold, for every stone

  • The purchase record for material bought as rough from independent miners, dated.
  • The laboratory report where one has been issued, with its number published openly on the stone's page so you can verify it at the laboratory rather than through us.
  • Export documentation and customs declarations for the shipment.
  • Our own record of which sourcing route the stone came through, marked on its catalogue entry: our own workings, an independent Mogok miner, or Namyarseik.

Trade bodies including the ICA and AGTA advise maintaining complete chain-of-custody documentation for any stone of potential Burmese origin. That is what the list above is for, and we will produce it on request rather than on demand.

Why there is no checkout on this website

The commercial arguments are real — an “add to cart” button beside a six-figure unheated ruby reframes it as a retail good, and fine coloured stones are negotiated rather than priced. But the decisive argument is this one.

An unattended checkout can accept an order from a jurisdiction where we may not lawfully or prudently transact, before any human has seen it. An enquiry-first model inserts the human review step by design rather than by policy. That is why we ask your country on the enquiry form, and why nothing on this site can be bought without a conversation.

Payment, shipping and insurance

Needs confirmation from SP Gems, and this is the section international buyers ask about first. Required: what payment routes are actually available to a Myanmar-registered entity receiving international funds; which carrier is used; what insurance cover applies and up to what value; which countries are served and which are not; who bears duty and import tax; and what happens if a stone is not as described on arrival.

We would rather say “we are working on the wording of this” than publish a confident paragraph that turns out to be wrong in your particular case.
