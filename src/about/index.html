About SP Gems

We are a gemstone mining, cutting and fine jewellery house, rooted in Mogok and headquartered in Yangon. If you are considering sending money to a company in Myanmar you have never met, this section exists to help you check us out rather than take our word for anything.

What we do

Four things, in this order. We operate our own workings in the Mogok valley. We buy rough from independent miners in Mogok and in Namyarseik, Kachin State. We cut everything ourselves in a workshop in Yangon, across the full range from commercial goods to exceptional single stones. And we make fine jewellery, under a designer-led collection called The Value.

The span is the point. Most houses selling Burmese material buy it already cut, from someone who bought it from someone else. We were there when it came out of the ground and we were there when it went on the wheel, which is why we can answer questions about a stone that a reseller cannot.

Where we are

No. 30A, Pyay Road (7 Mile), Mayangone Township, Yangon, Myanmar. That is a real address with a real building and we will show it to you on a video call if it helps.

How we sell

By conversation. There is no cart on this website and there will not be one. Fine coloured stones are priced against a live market, negotiated, and frequently shown before they are bought — and an unattended checkout on a six-figure stone would be wrong commercially and imprudent legally.

So every enquiry is read by a person, and the reply comes from a person. The full process is set out here, step by step, including the parts that are slow.

What we have removed from this page

Our previous About page was a single unbroken 350-word paragraph containing the entire company history, followed by two generic birthstone tables that had nothing to do with us or our customers. It also claimed clients including “some of the most recognized world brand, prominent celebrities and public figures,” named none of them, and described our materials as fair-trade without holding any certification.

All of that is gone. What remains is what we can support.
