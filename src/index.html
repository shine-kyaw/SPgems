The house

A family, a valley, and forty years of it

The SanPya family has traded precious gems in Mogok since 1985. SP Gems was formally established in 2007. We operate our own workings in the Mogok valley, buy additionally from miners we have known for years — including in Namyarseik, Kachin State — and cut everything in our own workshop in Yangon.

That vertical span is unusual. Most houses selling Burmese material buy it already cut, from someone who bought it from someone else.

Mogok

The valley that made the word “ruby” mean something

Mogok sits in a fold of marble in Upper Myanmar. The earliest surviving records of organised mining there date to 1597, and the valley has produced the reference standard for red ever since.

Mogok ruby has a gemmological signature: a strong red body colour, a red response under ultraviolet light caused by unusually low iron content, fine rutile silk, and a swirl-like internal growth structure. Those features are why a laboratory can call the origin, and why “Mogok” is worth more than “Burma”.

Sourcing

What we can document, and what we will not claim

We operate our own workings in Mogok and can document the origin of stones recovered from them. For material bought from other miners, we record the purchase at the point of sale. We keep purchase records, laboratory reports and export documentation for every stone we sell, and will produce them for a buyer on request.

We do not describe our material as fair-trade or conflict-free. Those terms have specific certification meanings we hold no certification against, and using them without one would be a claim we could not stand behind.
