The collection

Designed around the stone, not the other way round

Most jewellery is designed first and filled afterwards: a house settles on a silhouette, then buys stones to fit it. That is why so much of the market's material is calibrated — cut to a standard size so it can drop into a standard setting, with weight and colour sacrificed to the millimetre.

The Value works the other way. A stone comes off the wheel, and the question is what it should become. It is a slower process and it produces one-of-a-kind pieces rather than a line, which is a commercial disadvantage and an aesthetic advantage.

The collection is headed by Aung Kyaw Zin, who took an architecture degree in 2003 before turning to gem design. That background shows in the settings: the construction is worked out in section, not just in plan, and the gallery of a piece is designed as carefully as its face.
