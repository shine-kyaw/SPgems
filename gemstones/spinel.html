Mogok Spinel

For a thousand years spinel was sold as ruby, because nobody could tell them apart. Some of the most famous rubies in history are spinels. That history is now the stone’s advantage: it is almost never treated, it is singly refractive so its colour reads cleanly, and it comes out of the same Mogok marbles as our ruby.

About Mogok spinel

The stone that was mistaken for a better one

Spinel and ruby form in the same Mogok marbles, in the same colours, at the same time. Until the chemistry was worked out in the eighteenth century they were the same commodity. The distinction is real, though: ruby is corundum, spinel is a magnesium aluminium oxide, and spinel is singly refractive where ruby is doubly refractive. In practice that means a spinel shows one colour cleanly, with none of the dichroic shift a ruby carries.

Two things follow commercially. Spinel is almost never heated — there is no established treatment that improves it, so “unheated” is the normal state rather than a premium claim. And a fine red Mogok spinel above five carats is rarer in absolute terms than a ruby of the same size, while costing a fraction as much.

We also cut grey and violet-grey material from Namyarseik in Kachin State — a separate sourcing relationship from our Mogok operations. That colour has only recently found a market, and it is priced accordingly.
