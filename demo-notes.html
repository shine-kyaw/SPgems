Home/Demo notes

For SP Gems and the project team

What this build is

A design and content demo of the website specified in the SP Gems Website PRD v1.0. Its job is to settle the design system, the information architecture, the page templates and the data model — so that decisions get made against something real rather than against a description.

Read this first: there is no gemstone photography here

Not one image on this site is a photograph of a gemstone, and that is deliberate. §28.7 of the PRD prohibits stock photography and AI-generated imagery of gemstones or jewellery “under any circumstances,” on the grounds that in a category whose entire premise is authenticity, a synthetic image found by a customer would be commercially catastrophic. We agree, so we did not fake any.

Instead, every image slot renders a specimen plate: the gemmological neutral mid-grey ground specified in FR-CAT-040 (#8A8A8A), at the true aspect ratio the slot will use, carrying the shot specification for that slot. So the layout is honest about proportions and weight, and every placeholder doubles as a photography brief. Zoom into any stone's gallery and you will find the actual shot list.

Photography is the critical-path dependency for this project, and it is where the PRD expects it to fail. Twenty perfectly photographed stones beat two hundred poorly photographed ones, commercially and for the brand.

What is real

The design system Real and complete. Every colour token, the type scale, the spacing scale, the motion durations and easings, and the component rules are implemented exactly as specified in §28 — including the prohibitions: no gradients except a hero scrim, no glassmorphism, no shadows on cards, nothing above a 2px corner radius, no carousels for primary content, no pop-up newsletter. Lifts straight into the production build.
The information architecture Real. The six-item navigation of §9.2, the mega-menu structure of §9.3, and the URL structure of §10 including the gemstone slug pattern.
The data model Real. data/gemstones.json mirrors the complete field specification of §12.3 field for field, so it maps one-to-one onto the Payload CMS collection. Same for jewellery against §14.2.
The templates and behaviour Real and working: faceted filtering with URL state, live counts, disabled zero-result options, applied-filter chips, pagination, the diagnostic zero-result state, sorting, the full-screen viewer with all four zoom inputs, keyboard navigation and focus management, the contextual enquiry modal, the glossary disclosures, the multi-step forms with session persistence, and typo-tolerant search.
The editorial voice Real, and the thing most worth reviewing. The five education articles, the Mogok pages, and the sourcing and compliance pages are written as we would actually publish them.

What is not real

The inventory Twenty specimen stones and six specimen pieces. Structurally complete, commercially fictional. Certificate numbers use the literal placeholder pattern GRS20XX-XXXXXX and are deliberately not valid, verifiable report numbers — nothing here should ever be published.
All photography See above. Every image is a specimen plate carrying its own shot brief.
The forms No backend. Submitting renders the confirmation state with a reference number and logs the payload to the browser console. Nothing is sent and nothing is stored. File upload is not wired at all — it needs a server, and §15.4's security requirements are not optional.
The CMS Not present. Content is edited as JSON and HTML, and the site is generated by tools/build.ps1. Payload CMS is a production concern.
Seven of twelve articles Five of the twelve-article launch set are written out in full. The remaining seven appear on the Learn hub as the editorial plan, not as empty pages — §36.2 is explicit that a luxury site with placeholder pages is not a luxury site.

The stack, and why it is not the recommended one

§35 recommends Next.js with Payload CMS 3, PostgreSQL and object storage. That remains the right recommendation for production and nothing here argues against it.

This demo is static HTML, hand-written CSS and vanilla JavaScript, assembled by a PowerShell generator. Three reasons. It deploys to Vercel with no build step and no database, so a reviewer can look at it immediately. It needs no Node toolchain, which the machine this was built on does not have. And a demo's job is to settle design, IA and data-model questions — none of which are stack-dependent.

Everything here transfers. The CSS is a token file. The data is JSON matching the CMS field spec. The JavaScript is framework-free and describes the interaction contract precisely enough to reimplement as React components without guessing.

PRD decisions this build takes a position on

  • No cart, anywhere. Enquiry-first throughout, with exactly one visually primary CTA per detail page and messaging channels as clearly secondary options that carry the stone reference into the message.
  • Specifications public, three things gated. Carat, dimensions, shape, colour, clarity, origin region, treatment status, laboratory name and report number are all public. Full certificate scans, full-resolution imagery and per-carat pricing are not.
  • Price bands rather than pure POR. So buyers can self-qualify and so the price filter actually works. The exceptional tier stays on request.
  • Treatment disclosure on every page, equally prominent whether heated or not. This is the single most important trust decision in the build. A disclosure that only appears when the news is good is not a disclosure.
  • Three steps for commissions, not nine. All skippable, with a persistent escape hatch to the contact step.
  • Request-and-confirm consultations, not live booking. A booking system offering slots nobody attends is worse than an honest request form.
  • No invented content. No fabricated clients, testimonials, press, credentials, awards, collections or ethics claims. Where the PRD marks something needs confirmation, this build shows a visible note rather than plausible copy. Those notes are the shopping list.
  • Section 10 of the homepage is absent. Not an omission: FR-HOME-001 hides the news section automatically when the newest item is more than 120 days old. That rule is the structural fix for a site whose latest news is a sale from 2017, still written in the future tense.

Things the current site should fix this week, regardless

§4.4 lists seven defects visible to every visitor today that cost hours rather than weeks. Five are worth doing immediately and independently of this project: the social links pointing at the literal string http://url; the footer reading “All right reserved 2015” and misspelling “Jewellery”; the 2017 sale still promoted in the present tense; the live WooCommerce login with no products behind it and no legitimate users; and the two Gmail addresses handling all correspondence.

What we need from SP Gems, in order

  1. Photography

    The critical path. Commission a photography standard document and a pilot shoot of twenty stones before development begins. Neutral mid-grey ground, colour reference target in the calibration frame, 4000px minimum on the long edge, three images minimum per stone and five to eight as the target, with a second lighting condition for ruby, sapphire and spinel. Then answer: does product photography already exist, at what resolution, who owns the rights, and is there in-house capability?

  2. Inventory data

    Sixty stones minimum and fifteen jewellery pieces at launch. Below roughly forty the catalogue does not feel like a collection and the filters have nothing to do. Every stone needs the full §12.3 record — and treatment status is non-negotiable: publishing is blocked without it.

  3. Company identity

    Registered name, company number, jurisdiction. A professional domain email to replace the Gmail addresses. Named team members with photographs, including whoever answers enquiries. Any gemmological credentials held by anyone in the business — if they exist they are a significant and currently unstated asset.

  4. Legal and compliance input

    Current professional advice on which markets can be served and on what documentary basis. Counsel review of the sourcing page, the international purchasing page, the privacy notice, the terms and the cookie policy. This blocks launch content, not development start.

  5. Process answers

    What payment routes are actually available. Which carrier and what insurance cover. Which countries are served. The real commission process, its timeline and its deposit terms. Business hours, working days and holiday closures. Every one of these is a question a buyer asks in their first two emails.

  6. Collection decisions

    Is “The Value” a collection, a sub-brand, or the jewellery house name? This materially affects the information architecture. Which pieces are the eight uncaptioned images on the current site, and are they still available? Does the Lilac Flower Collection exist — it is referenced once, in a video caption, with no page, images or description.

  7. Operational ownership

    The one that quietly kills projects like this. Who at SP Gems photographs stones, writes listings, marks them sold, and answers enquiries — in what language and how quickly? A catalogue that goes stale is worse than no catalogue, and the 2017 content freeze on the current site is the evidence that this needs an owner rather than good intentions.

Working on this build

Sources live in src/, shared chrome in tools/partials/, content in data/*.json. Run tools/build.ps1 to regenerate; output is written to the repository root and committed so Vercel serves it directly. Detail pages for stones and jewellery are generated from the JSON, so adding a stone is a data edit and a rebuild.

To preview locally, tools/serve.ps1 starts a static server on port 8787.

The entire placeholder system lives in assets/css/plates.css and can be deleted wholesale when real photography arrives. Nothing in site.css depends on it.
